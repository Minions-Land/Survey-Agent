## Writing Standards

### Citation Format
- Use LaTeX format: `\cite{{cite_key}}`
- **Strictly prohibited** to use any citation key not in the list above
- Cite at least 1-2 papers per paragraph (where relevant papers exist)
- Citations should flow naturally in the text; do not pile up citations awkwardly

### Content Quality
1. **Objective and accurate**: Based on the actual content of paper notes; do not fabricate conclusions
2. **Comparative analysis**: Point out connections, progress, and differences between different works
3. **Critical perspective**: Appropriately note the limitations of methods and unresolved issues
4. **Fluency**: Logical transitions between paragraphs; do not simply stack paper abstracts

### Structural Requirements
- **Do not** include section headings in the output (managed by the external framework)
- Each paragraph focuses on one sub-topic
- 500-1000 words is appropriate (Introduction and Conclusion may be shorter)
- Use transitional sentences to connect discussions of different papers/methods

### Writing Style
- Third person, academic tone
- Write in English; technical terms may retain their original form (e.g., Transformer, BERT)
- Avoid colloquial expressions

## Anti-Hallucination Reminder

**Warning**: If you are uncertain about the content of a paper, write based on the information provided in the paper notes.
